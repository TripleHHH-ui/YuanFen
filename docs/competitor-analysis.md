# Competitor analysis — the whitespace

Positioning axes: **taste-blind → knows your taste** (vertical), **waits for a search → moves first** (horizontal). Everyone else clusters bottom/left; the open quadrant — knows your taste *and* moves first *and* books — is empty.

| Who | What they nail | What they structurally cannot do |
|---|---|---|
| **Mindtrip** | Chat + map + itinerary in one surface; backed by airline and bank strategics; ~350K monthly US visitors in ~6 months | Preferences come from a quiz and chat history, not a swipe taste graph. No proactive fare layer. Itinerary does not re-plan around the flight. |
| **OTA / metasearch AI** (Trip.com Trip.Planner & TripGenie, Expedia Romie, Kayak AI Mode) | Convert intent you already have into bookings, at massive scale | None manufactures intent from a calendar + taste profile + fare board — the itinerary and the fare stay separate objects. |
| **Tryp.com** | Ready-made deals tailored to location, filterable (Weekend/Beach), priced off cheapest dates, bookings in one timeline; closed a $3M+ round in 2025 | Taste-blind and calendar-blind — pushes whatever is cheapest from your city, no ground itinerary, no map, no learned preference. |
| **Google Gemini (Personal Intelligence)** | Proactively factors in interests across Gmail/Photos/Search/YouTube; pulls live Maps/Flights/Hotels data; announced ~2 weeks before this brief | A general assistant you summon, not a product that moves first on a fare-and-holiday trigger. Won't rank LCC fares by total-cost-with-bag or reflow a day plan when you swap flights. |
| **Kayak Explore / Google Flights Explore** | Beautiful fare-map browsing from your city | Zero idea who you are — a price on a map, never a planned trip. |
| **Hopper** | Price advice and watch alerts people trust, huge install base | You must pick and watch the route first; advice is a prediction claim; no ground itinerary exists at all. |
| **Going** | Proactive, human-vetted deal push from your home airports | Deal-driven rather than calendar- or taste-driven; hands you a link, not a trip. |
| **Wanderlog and classic planners** | Manual itinerary/map organization, route optimizer | Everything typed in by hand — nothing learned, nothing moves first, nothing books. |
| **Swipe-deck "Tinder for travel" apps** (OfftheGrid, Laika, Tourder, Flytinary) | Made swipe itself the whole product | Category has repeatedly stayed tiny or died — because swipe with no payoff downstream is a dead end. Ours treats swipe as an input device whose payoff (the route, the deal hand) shows up elsewhere — the Pinterest pattern, not the Tinder-clone pattern. |

## The honest calibration

Every ingredient here is borrowed — a judge can name the source of each one, and claiming total originality invites exactly that takedown. **What's ours alone is the closed loop**: taste learned by swiping → a holiday calendar + nightly fare board deciding when to speak → a pre-built itinerary attached to the fare → gated agent booking → downstream re-planning. That full loop doesn't exist in any shipped product found across two independent research passes.

Say **"first to close the loop"** or **"the missing quadrant"** on stage — not "one of a kind." On the judges' own 4-point scale: chat-to-itinerary alone is a 1–2 (Mindtrip owns that surface), the concept as a whole is a solid 3, and the reflow + unprompted alert, executed on real Atlas data, is what argues for a 4. Sequence the demo so the alert firing unprompted and the flight-swap reflow read as the headline; the chat-planning scene is table stakes we also happen to have.
